Agentic AI stopped being a buzzword the moment the checkbooks came out. In the past year, the world's biggest tech companies have spent tens of billions of dollars buying (or funding) the "doers" of AI — systems that don't just chat, but actually complete tasks, resolve tickets, secure networks, and draft legal briefs on their own.

Here are five deals that show where the money — and the momentum — is really going.

## 1. Salesforce → Fin (formerly Intercom) — $3.6 Billion

Salesforce signed a definitive agreement to acquire Fin, the customer-service AI agent company formerly known as Intercom, in a deal expected to close in Q4 of Salesforce's fiscal 2027 ([Salesforce press release](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2026/06/15/salesforce-signs-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-fin/)).

Fin's AI agent resolves complex customer queries end-to-end across channels like live chat, email, WhatsApp, SMS, phone, and Slack. The deal is designed to plug straight into Salesforce's flagship Agentforce platform, which had already hit $1.2 billion in annual recurring revenue in Q1 FY27, up 205% year-over-year. Beyond the tech, Salesforce also picked up a customer base of more than 30,000 companies and a technical AI team with deep customer-service automation experience ([CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/customer-experience/salesforce-acquires-fin/)).

It's a bet that owning a proven, high-resolution-rate agent is worth more than building one from scratch — even as some analysts questioned whether Salesforce's rapid string of AI acquisitions is starting to look scattered ([CIO.com](https://www.cio.com/article/4185285/salesforce-buying-agentic-ai-firm-fin-for-3-6-billion.html)).

## 2. ServiceNow's "AI Control Tower" Spree — $2.85B (Moveworks) + $7.75B (Armis)

ServiceNow didn't make one big move — it made several, methodically assembling what CEO Bill McDermott calls an "AI control tower." The company closed its $2.85 billion acquisition of agentic AI provider Moveworks, whose AI assistant now plays a role in securing autonomous workflows, before going on to acquire identity-security platform Veza and, in its largest deal yet, cybersecurity company Armis for $7.75 billion ([Dark Reading](https://www.darkreading.com/cybersecurity-operations/servicenow-buys-armis-gets-ai-control-tower)).

McDermott summed up the strategy plainly: "ServiceNow will have the only AI control tower that drives workflow, action and business outcomes across all of these environments" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/23/servicenow-armis-cybersecurity-acquisition.html)). The combined deals are expected to more than triple ServiceNow's addressable market for security and risk solutions ([ServiceNow Newsroom](https://newsroom.servicenow.com/press-releases/details/2026/ServiceNow-completes-Armis-acquisition-closing-the-gap-between-asset-visibility-and-cyber-risk/default.aspx)).

## 3. Google/Alphabet → Wiz — $32 Billion

The single largest deal on this list — and the biggest acquisition in Google's history. Google closed its $32 billion all-cash acquisition of cloud security platform Wiz on March 11, 2026, after a year-long global regulatory review that included unconditional clearance from the U.S., EU, and several other jurisdictions ([Cleary Gottlieb](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/news-and-insights/news-listing/google-completes-32-billion-acquisition-of-wiz)).

While Wiz itself isn't an "agent" in the conversational sense, the deal is squarely about securing the agentic AI era: Wiz's cloud-native platform scans an organization's entire footprint across multiple clouds to catch the kind of toxic risk combinations that autonomous, always-on AI systems could otherwise expose ([Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4563161-google-closes-32b-deal-to-acquire-wiz)). It's a reminder that as agents get more autonomy, someone has to build the guardrails — and Google just paid a record price to own that layer.

## 4. Meta → Manus — Over $2 Billion

Meta closed out 2025 by acquiring Manus, a Singapore-based, Chinese-founded startup specializing in agentic AI for small and medium-sized businesses, in a deal reported at over $2 billion ([CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/news/business/meta-manus-acquisition-two-billion-explained-9.7030180)).

Manus had built a general-purpose AI agent that autonomously carries out multi-step tasks — research, analysis, coding, planning, content generation — using cloud-based virtual machines to sandbox and execute work without losing progress across long, complex sessions ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/why-meta-bought-manus-and-what-it-means-for-your-enterprise-ai-agent)). The deal is widely read as Meta buying the "execution layer" it lacked, giving its Llama models a path to actually do things rather than just talk about them.

## 5. Legora — $550 Million Series D (Legal AI)

Not every big agentic AI story is an acquisition — some are pure funding rounds. Legora, a Stockholm-founded collaborative AI platform for lawyers, raised $550 million in a Series D round led by Accel, valuing the company at $5.55 billion, roughly tripling its valuation from just five months earlier ([Legora Newsroom](https://legora.com/newsroom/legora-raises-550-million-series-d-to-fuel-us-growth)).

The round included nearly every major VC name in the game — Benchmark, Bessemer, General Catalyst, ICONIQ, Redpoint, Y Combinator, plus new backers like Bain Capital and Salesforce Ventures. As Accel partner Arun Mathew put it, "work is quickly shifting to end-to-end workflows run by agents, and more of that work is happening on Legora" ([Legora Newsroom](https://legora.com/newsroom/legora-raises-550-million-series-d-to-fuel-us-growth)). Legora's platform is used by tens of thousands of lawyers across more than 800 law firms in over 50 markets — proof that agentic AI isn't just a big-tech story, it's rewriting entire professional industries.

## The Bigger Picture

Five deals, five very different bets — customer service, IT/security operations, cloud infrastructure, general-purpose task execution, and legal work. What ties them together is simple: the market has decided that agentic AI's value isn't in talking anymore. It's in doing. And right now, the world's biggest companies are paying billions to own the "doing."
